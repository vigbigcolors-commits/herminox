From breakeven ACoS to a TACoS ceiling you can defend

ACoS alone lies when organic share rises. TACoS is ad spend over total sales — so the same campaign ACoS becomes cheaper for the business as organic grows. You need both numbers.

Worked example

50% organic. Pre-ad margin implies 40% breakeven ACoS. At 50% organic share, TACoS at that ceiling ≈ 40% × 0.50 = 20%. Running 25% ACoS on ad sales with 50% organic → TACoS ≈ 12.5% — room under the 20% ceiling. Same 25% ACoS at 10% organic → TACoS ≈ 22.5%, which can sit above a thin total-margin plan.

Launch vs harvest is a TACoS story

New listings often have low organic share. A “high” ACoS can still be rational if you are buying rank with a time box — but TACoS will look painful because almost every sale is ad-attributed. Mature listings should pull ACoS down so TACoS stays well under the breakeven-derived ceiling.

Never copy a competitor’s ACoS target without knowing their organic mix and pre-ad margin.

Build the ceiling in order

  1. Get pre-ad profit from real fees (FBA calculator).
  2. Breakeven ACoS = pre-ad margin %.
  3. Estimate organic share honestly (not aspirational).
  4. TACoS ceiling ≈ BE ACoS × (1 − organic).
  5. Set campaign targets below that ceiling — often 50–75% of BE ACoS on ad sales for profit mode.

Checklist

  • Pre-ad margin known
  • Breakeven ACoS written down
  • Organic share estimated from reports
  • TACoS ceiling computed
  • Campaign target set below ceiling
  • Revisit when organic share changes

FAQ

Can TACoS be higher than ACoS?

Not under standard definitions — total sales ≥ ad sales, so TACoS ≤ ACoS when both use the same spend.

What organic share should I assume at launch?

Low — often under 20% early. Using 60% “someday” organic to justify today’s spend is how launches bleed.

Is breakeven ACoS a target?

No. It is a ceiling. Targets usually sit meaningfully below it so returns and bid noise do not erase profit.
